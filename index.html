Multiciliated cells reuse cell-cycle machinery without completing a canonical cell cycle. The Choksi Lab's work identifies a sequence—G1/G0-like entry, S-like centriole amplification, G2/M-like maturation, then a second G1/G0-like state—and shows that CDK4/6 and E2F7 separate productive multiciliation from proliferation.

I turned that model into a small decision tool: choose the biological uncertainty, inspect the stage-specific evidence, and receive a proposed perturbation, orthogonal readouts, controls, and a go/no-go rule for a focused mTEC experiment.

How it works

The evidence layer comes from the 2024 Nature study and its public GSE245381 subseries: 12 single-cell libraries—six DMSO and six ribociclib—generated as three replicates on each of two sequencing platforms. The decision layer is my explicit 10-point rubric for evidence, readout tractability, information gain, and speed. Its score is a planning aid, not a statistic reported by the authors.

The two highest-priority plans begin with causal anchors already established in the paper: CDK4/6 inhibition reduced multiciliated-cell differentiation in three biological replicates (reported P = 0.0118 and 0.0183 for the two inhibitors), while E2F7 loss uncoupled DNA synthesis from normal centriole maturation and ciliogenesis. CDK1 localization and cyclin B1 timing remain valuable, but are presented as lower-confidence mechanism-discovery leads.

Why this matters for the lab

The tool makes the handoff between single-cell evidence and bench validation explicit. It favors experiments that can distinguish a blocked fate transition from general toxicity or cell-cycle re-entry, and it pairs transcript-level markers with imaging-level structural outcomes. That is the kind of decision support I would want before spending a week of primary airway culture on a perturbation.

What I would bring

I can contribute on both sides of this workflow: maintaining careful sample and colony records, processing mouse tissue and cell cultures, running flow or microscopy-based readouts, and using Python/R to turn single-cell and quantitative imaging data into reproducible comparisons. I would be especially useful in keeping experimental metadata, image-derived phenotypes, and scRNA-seq results aligned around the same biological question.
